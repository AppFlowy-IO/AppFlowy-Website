If you are comparing AppFlowy vs Confluence for an enterprise team, the right choice depends on two questions: where must the workspace run, and how much structured operational work must it manage alongside documentation?

The short answer: choose Confluence when your organization is moving to Atlassian Cloud, depends heavily on Jira and Marketplace apps, or wants Confluence's mature wiki and whiteboard experience. Choose AppFlowy when long-term self-hosting, air-gapped deployment, local or on-premises AI, native desktop apps, and relational databases are central requirements.

Both products support multi-user real-time collaboration, hierarchical knowledge, comments, permissions, version history, databases, mobile access, and AI. The meaningful differences are deployment control, ecosystem depth, and the way each product connects documents to structured data.

## What is the biggest difference between AppFlowy and Confluence?

The biggest difference is the operating model.

Confluence is a mature knowledge platform built around the Atlassian ecosystem. Its strongest path is Atlassian Cloud, where teams can combine Confluence pages, live docs, whiteboards, databases, Jira work items, Rovo, and Marketplace extensions. For organizations already standardized on Jira and other Atlassian products, this connected ecosystem can be a decisive advantage.

AppFlowy is an all-in-one workspace designed to run in the cloud or on infrastructure the organization controls. It combines documents and hierarchical wikis with relational databases, multiple operational views, forms, charts, and AI that can run locally or on-premises. This makes AppFlowy attractive to teams that want to reduce infrastructure dependency while keeping knowledge and execution in one workspace.

The choice is therefore not simply modern interface versus established wiki. It is cloud ecosystem depth versus deployment flexibility and a more integrated document-and-database model.

## What does Confluence Data Center end of life mean?

[Atlassian has scheduled Data Center end of life](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life) for March 28, 2029 at 23:59 PST. At that point, affected Data Center subscriptions and associated Marketplace apps enter a read-only state under the standard lifecycle.

The most important dates are:

| Date           | Data Center milestone                                                                                                |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| March 30, 2026 | New customers could no longer purchase new Data Center subscriptions or associated apps.                             |
| March 30, 2028 | The standard window closes for existing customers to purchase new Data Center apps or expand existing subscriptions. |
| March 28, 2029 | Affected Data Center subscriptions and Marketplace apps reach end of life and become read-only.                      |

Atlassian describes limited extended-maintenance arrangements for eligible customers, so organizations with exceptional constraints should confirm their exact options directly with Atlassian. Even so, the published standard path is clear: Confluence Cloud is Atlassian's long-term platform, while Confluence Data Center is not a permanent self-hosting option.

This creates a strategic decision for infrastructure-conscious organizations:

- move the workspace and its integrations to Atlassian Cloud
- keep Data Center during the transition and plan a later migration
- adopt a platform with an ongoing self-hosted roadmap

AppFlowy addresses the third path. It can run through Docker Compose or Kubernetes in a private cloud, on-premises environment, or fully air-gapped network. The organization controls networking, storage, authentication, backups, AI services, and upgrade policy.

Self-hosting still creates operational responsibilities. Infrastructure teams must plan availability, monitoring, backups, disaster recovery, security updates, and capacity. The benefit is control, not the absence of work.

## Can AppFlowy replace Confluence?

For many common Confluence workflows, yes. AppFlowy can support company knowledge, project hubs, policies, meeting notes, collaborative documentation, operational databases, comments, permissions, and version history.

Replacement suitability depends on what surrounds the pages. An organization that primarily uses Confluence for documentation and lightweight workflows may have a direct path. A team with years of custom macros, Jira automation, specialized Marketplace apps, embedded third-party systems, and bespoke Atlassian reporting should expect more discovery and redesign.

The practical question is not whether AppFlowy can display text that once lived in Confluence. It is whether the target workspace can preserve or improve every business process that depends on that content.

Before deciding, inventory:

- spaces, page trees, live docs, attachments, comments, and templates
- global, space, and content-level permissions
- guests and external sharing rules
- Jira links, automation, dashboards, and reports
- custom macros and Marketplace apps
- databases, page properties, and recurring workflows
- SSO, provisioning, directory, and audit requirements
- retention, backup, export, and compliance policies

## How do collaboration and knowledge management compare?

Both AppFlowy and Confluence support multi-user real-time collaboration. Neither should be evaluated as a single-user offline notebook.

Confluence is especially strong as an established organizational wiki. Teams can organize pages into spaces, create reusable page templates, use inline and page comments, manage restrictions, publish updates, and connect knowledge closely to Jira. Confluence Cloud also supports live docs, databases, and whiteboards alongside traditional pages.

AppFlowy supports collaborative documents, hierarchical pages, comments, mentions, version history, wikis, and concurrent database editing. Native desktop and mobile clients can connect to the same team workspace, while offline-capable clients add resilience for travel, unreliable networks, and desktop-first work.

For external collaboration, both products provide controlled guest models. Confluence guests are generally assigned to one space and remain subject to that space's content restrictions. AppFlowy can invite guests to selected content and supports guest invite approval workflows. In either product, enterprises should test the exact experience for contractors, customers, agencies, and cross-company stakeholders.

Knowledge-management takeaway: Confluence has the more mature wiki ecosystem and tighter Atlassian context. AppFlowy connects comparable team knowledge workflows to a broader native database and project layer.

## How do AppFlowy and Confluence databases compare?

Confluence Cloud databases are more capable than a simple table. According to Atlassian's [database documentation](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/get-started-with-confluence-databases/), teams can create typed fields, link entries, filter and sort data, save views, and present a database in Table, Card, or Board layouts.

AppFlowy also stores structured records, but its database model is designed to cover more operational use cases. It provides Grid, Kanban, Calendar, Gallery, List, Timeline, Feed, Chart, and Form views, together with linked views, full-page records, record templates, two-way relations, rollups, formulas, and calculations.

The difference becomes clearer in three areas: linked views, working pages, and relational reporting.

### Linked views: a precise comparison

Confluence can reuse one data source. A team can embed an entire database, a saved view, an entry, or an individual value through a dynamic Smart Link. Multiple saved views can live on the source database, and embedded content stays synchronized with that source.

AppFlowy takes a different approach. Each linked database instance can point to the same source records while keeping its own filters, sorts, groups, visible properties, and layout. The destination context does not need to add every audience-specific configuration to the source database's shared view list.

Consider one central Projects database:

| Destination             | AppFlowy linked-view configuration                            |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Engineering workspace   | Kanban filtered to the Engineering team and grouped by status |
| Leadership dashboard    | Grid filtered to strategic projects and sorted by risk        |
| Quarterly planning page | Timeline filtered to the current quarter                      |
| Customer delivery hub   | Calendar filtered to committed external milestones            |

All four instances operate on the same records. Each audience gets a useful presentation without copying the data.

This distinction matters for executive dashboards, department homepages, project hubs, OKR reviews, and cross-functional planning. Confluence supports connected embeds and saved source views; AppFlowy provides more independent configuration for each linked instance.

### Full-page records: structured data and working context together

In AppFlowy, every database record is also a full page. A Project record can contain structured properties such as owner, status, priority, risk, and due date together with the project's objectives, scope, requirements, milestones, decisions, comments, and weekly updates.

Confluence database entries can link to pages or live docs for detailed content. Teams can create those separate content items from page templates. That model works, but it introduces a distinction between the structured entry and its working document.

AppFlowy keeps both layers in one record. A database template can be applied directly when the record is created, so each new project, incident, risk, vendor, or customer account starts with the expected properties and document structure.

| Workflow          | Structured properties                           | Content inside the AppFlowy record                              |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Project           | Owner, status, priority, dates, risk            | Objectives, requirements, milestones, decisions, weekly updates |
| Incident          | Severity, service, responder, state, timestamps | Timeline, impact, investigation, remediation, retrospective     |
| Security review   | System, owner, reviewer, control status         | Evidence, threat model, exceptions, approvals                   |
| Customer account  | Segment, owner, renewal, health                 | Discovery notes, decisions, success plan, meeting history       |
| Vendor evaluation | Category, cost, risk, decision                  | Requirements, security review, legal notes, recommendation      |

The benefit is not that Confluence cannot model these workflows. It can. The benefit is fewer objects to maintain, less context switching, and a direct way to standardize the working page attached to every record.

### Relations, rollups, and automatic reporting

Confluence can connect databases with Entry links and backlinks. Its Entry details field can surface values from linked records, and [simple calculations](https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/perform-simple-calculations-on-your-data/) can summarize a field within a database view with operations such as sum, average, minimum, maximum, and count.

AppFlowy adds a relational rollup model. A database can relate its records to another database and automatically aggregate a property across those related records. Formulas can then transform or evaluate the result.

For example:

- a Project can roll up task count, completed effort, latest due date, or open risk
- a Portfolio can summarize status and budget across related projects
- an Account can aggregate open opportunities and pipeline value
- a Vendor can summarize contract value, renewal dates, and unresolved reviews
- an Objective can calculate progress from related key results
- an Incident dashboard can count affected services by severity

Confluence's view calculations answer questions about the entries visible in one view. AppFlowy rollups answer questions about the records related to each parent record. That is an important distinction for portfolio reporting, CRM-style workflows, procurement, OKRs, budgets, hiring, and other cross-database operations.

Database takeaway: Confluence provides useful native structured data inside a mature wiki. AppFlowy is stronger when relational data is part of the operating system of the team rather than an enhancement to documentation.

## Which product is better for enterprise permissions and identity?

Both platforms provide meaningful enterprise governance, but their models and packaging differ.

Confluence Cloud uses global permissions, space permissions, and content restrictions. Administrators can assign product and app roles, control access to spaces, restrict individual pages, manage guests, and review audit information. Organizations can add SAML single sign-on and SCIM user provisioning through [Atlassian Guard](https://www.atlassian.com/software/guard/pricing).

AppFlowy supports workspace membership, Custom Spaces, Groups, page-level controls, external guests, and guest invite requests. Custom Spaces can restrict access to selected members or Groups, helping organizations separate departments, projects, clients, and business units. AppFlowy's enterprise identity options include SAML, OIDC, SCIM, and LDAP, together with security audit logs in its self-hosted offering.

Space-level permissions are important because they create an administrative boundary above individual pages. IT and workspace administrators can establish who belongs in Finance, Legal, Engineering, a customer account, or a confidential initiative, then manage page-level exceptions within that boundary.

Governance takeaway: Confluence has a sophisticated cloud permission model and strong Atlassian administration. AppFlowy is compelling when those controls must run on organization-managed infrastructure or integrate with LDAP, OIDC, and a self-hosted operating model.

## How do AppFlowy AI and Confluence Rovo compare?

Both products offer useful AI. The main difference is where the AI can run.

[Confluence Rovo](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/ai) provides search, chat, agents, writing assistance, summaries, meeting insights, and connected knowledge across Atlassian Cloud. Rovo answers can cite their sources and respect a user's permissions. For an organization already using Jira, Confluence, and connected SaaS applications, this creates a broad cloud knowledge layer.

AppFlowy provides AI search, writing and chat, meeting notes, audio and video transcripts, and database assistance. It can connect to AppFlowy's managed AI or use local and self-hosted LLM and embedding models. Enterprises can keep prompts, embeddings, transcripts, and generated responses inside their security boundary, including in restricted-network or air-gapped environments.

This produces a straightforward decision rule:

- choose Confluence Rovo when cloud-based intelligence across the Atlassian ecosystem is the priority
- choose AppFlowy when AI must operate locally, on-premises, or inside infrastructure the organization controls

AI quality also depends on the selected models, document quality, permissions, indexing, and retrieval configuration. Teams should test representative questions with sensitive and restricted content before rollout.

## Which platform has better integrations?

Confluence has the stronger integration ecosystem. Its native relationship with Jira, Atlassian automation, Rovo connectors, and thousands of Marketplace apps is difficult to match. Teams can extend Confluence with diagrams, reporting, workflow, compliance, publishing, and industry-specific tools.

AppFlowy supports public APIs, MCP, Zapier, Google Drive, Google Calendar, YouTube, and other integrations, but it does not offer the same Marketplace breadth. Its alternative advantage is native scope: documents, relational data, forms, charts, and project views can live in one product instead of being assembled from several extensions.

Ecosystem takeaway: choose Confluence when specialized Atlassian integrations are a core dependency. Choose AppFlowy when consolidating the workspace and reducing add-on dependence is more important.

## Which has better apps and visual collaboration?

AppFlowy provides native applications for Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android in addition to its web app. This is useful for desktop-first teams and organizations that want a consistent client experience across managed devices.

Confluence is primarily web-based and provides native iOS and Android apps. It does not provide a native desktop application. Confluence does, however, include whiteboards that connect visual planning with Jira and Confluence content.

The tradeoff is clear: AppFlowy offers broader native platform coverage, while Confluence offers a stronger integrated whiteboard experience.

## How should enterprises compare total cost of ownership?

Subscription price alone does not capture the cost of either platform.

For Confluence Cloud, model the required plan, Atlassian Guard, Marketplace apps, Jira or other Atlassian products, storage, migration, and administration. Ecosystem depth can lower integration effort, but a large app portfolio can also increase licensing and governance complexity.

For self-hosted AppFlowy, model infrastructure, observability, backups, disaster recovery, security patching, upgrades, support, and the time required from platform engineers. Self-hosting can improve control and reduce vendor dependence, but those benefits come with operational ownership.

A useful total-cost comparison includes:

- software subscriptions and add-ons
- identity and security services
- infrastructure and storage
- implementation and migration
- integration maintenance
- administration and user support
- backup, recovery, and compliance operations
- the cost of switching again if the platform no longer fits

The lower-cost choice depends on the organization. A deeply Atlassian-based team may gain more from Confluence Cloud's integration efficiency. A regulated organization with existing Kubernetes, identity, and observability platforms may gain more from AppFlowy's deployment control and consolidation.

## Where Confluence may fit better

Choose Confluence when:

- your organization is committed to Atlassian Cloud
- Jira is the center of product, engineering, or service-management work
- Marketplace apps and custom Atlassian workflows are business-critical
- a mature wiki and page-centric knowledge system are the main requirements
- integrated whiteboards are important
- Rovo across cloud knowledge sources fits your AI policy
- a single Atlassian administration and billing environment is preferable

Confluence's depth is a strength, although it can create a steeper learning curve as spaces, permissions, macros, databases, Jira links, automation, and apps accumulate. For complex deployments, information architecture and governance are as important as product configuration.

## How should an enterprise migrate from Confluence to AppFlowy?

A successful migration begins with workflow discovery, not a bulk export.

1. Inventory the current environment. Record spaces, page trees, databases, attachments, templates, macros, Jira links, apps, permissions, guests, identity settings, and retention rules.
2. Classify dependencies. Separate portable documents from content that depends on macros, Jira, Marketplace apps, custom scripts, or Confluence-specific metadata.
3. Design the target model. Decide which spaces become AppFlowy Spaces, which page collections remain documents, and which operational content should become relational databases.
4. Migrate a representative pilot. Include complex pages, attachments, nested content, restricted areas, database records, and external collaborators.
5. Validate semantics, not only appearance. Test links, authorship, comments, permissions, attachments, database properties, search, and unsupported blocks. Export formats do not preserve every source-system behavior.
6. Rebuild workflows deliberately. Use full-page records, database templates, linked views, relations, and rollups where they simplify a process previously split across pages and add-ons.
7. Roll out by department or workspace. Keep rollback options and a clear cutover window instead of moving the entire organization at once.

AppFlowy supports Confluence import workflows and provides high-touch migration assistance for enterprise customers. A pilot should still use real organizational content because migration risk is usually hidden in edge cases, permissions, and integrations rather than ordinary text pages.

## Final verdict

Confluence remains an excellent choice for organizations moving to Atlassian Cloud and building around Jira, Marketplace apps, whiteboards, and Rovo. Its knowledge-management maturity and ecosystem are genuine advantages.

AppFlowy is the stronger choice when the organization needs a long-term self-hosted Confluence alternative, wants AI inside its own security boundary, or needs documents and relational operations to behave as one system. Independent linked views, full-page database records, direct record templates, relations, and rollups make it especially suitable for projects, portfolios, incidents, risks, accounts, vendors, compliance reviews, OKRs, hiring, and recurring business processes.

The best decision comes from testing the workflows that are hardest to move: permissions, identity, Jira dependencies, database relationships, reporting, AI boundaries, and external collaboration.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is AppFlowy better than Confluence?

AppFlowy is better for teams that prioritize long-term self-hosting, air-gapped deployment, local or on-premises AI, native desktop apps, and advanced relational workflows. Confluence may be better for organizations committed to Atlassian Cloud, Jira, Marketplace apps, Rovo, and integrated whiteboards.

### Can AppFlowy replace Confluence Data Center?

AppFlowy can replace many Confluence Data Center workflows, including hierarchical documentation, collaborative pages, permissions, comments, version history, databases, project hubs, and knowledge search. Organizations should separately assess custom macros, Jira automation, Marketplace apps, and bespoke integrations before migrating.

### When does Confluence Data Center reach end of life?

Atlassian's standard Data Center lifecycle ends on March 28, 2029 at 23:59 PST. Affected subscriptions and associated Marketplace apps become read-only. Atlassian documents limited extended-maintenance arrangements for eligible customers, so confirm exceptional circumstances directly with Atlassian.

### Do AppFlowy and Confluence both support real-time collaboration?

Yes. Both support multi-user real-time work, comments, permissions, and version history. AppFlowy supports concurrent document and database editing, while Confluence supports collaborative pages, live docs, databases, and other cloud content.

### Does Confluence have databases?

Yes. Confluence Cloud databases support typed fields, filters, sorting, saved views, linked entries, and Table, Card, and Board layouts. AppFlowy adds more layouts plus full-page records, direct record templates, independent linked instances, relations, rollups, and formulas.

### Does Confluence support linked database views?

Confluence can embed an entire database or a saved source view, and the embedded content remains connected to the source. AppFlowy linked instances can each keep independent filters, sorts, groups, visible properties, and layouts while referencing the same records.

### What is the difference between Confluence calculations and AppFlowy rollups?

### Which product has better AI?

### Which product integrates better with Jira?

Confluence integrates more deeply with Jira and the broader Atlassian ecosystem. Organizations whose processes depend on Jira-native workflows, automation, and Marketplace apps should treat that as a major advantage during evaluation.

### Can AppFlowy run in an air-gapped environment?

Yes. AppFlowy supports private-cloud, on-premises, and fully air-gapped deployment. Organizations can also use local or self-hosted LLM and embedding models so AI processing remains inside the controlled environment.

### Does Confluence have a desktop app?

### How should teams test a Confluence migration?
